## 1. Product Summary

BookTracker is a cross-platform reading companion — available as a web app and iOS/Android mobile app — that lets readers track their library, log reading progress, discover stats about their habits, and discuss books inside private clubs. Key differentiators:

- **Frictionless onboarding:** Import an entire reading history from Goodreads or a spreadsheet in one step; covers and metadata fill in automatically.
- **Barcode & ISBN lookup:** Scan the back of any book to add it instantly, no typing required.
- **Book-set import:** Paste or scan a whole box-set of ISBNs at once, or scan a single boxed-set ISBN and let the app guess the individual volumes — then mark each one individually or bulk-update them all.
- **Book clubs built-in:** Private, invite-only clubs with shared reading lists and threaded discussion — no third-party app needed.
- **Dark mode everywhere:** Light, dark, and system themes on both web and mobile, with a consistent, comfortable reading experience at any hour.
- **Multilingual from day one:** Full English and German UI with in-app language switching.

## 2. Target Audience

### Primary — The Dedicated Reader
- **Who:** Adults 22–45 who read 12+ books per year and already track reads informally (Notes app, spreadsheet, Goodreads).
- **Pain:** Goodreads is slow, cluttered, and owned by Amazon; spreadsheets lack a mobile experience; no single tool combines tracking + social.
- **Motivation:** Wants a clean, private space to log reading and see progress over time.

### Secondary — The Book Club Organizer
- **Who:** One person per 5–10-person friend group who organizes the club, picks books, and drives discussion.
- **Pain:** Coordination happens across WhatsApp, email, and a shared doc — scattered and forgettable.
- **Motivation:** One place for the whole club: the book list, progress, and conversation.

### Tertiary — The Commuter/Casual Reader
- **Who:** Reads 3–6 books per year, mostly on mobile.
- **Pain:** Can never remember what they've read or what was recommended to them.
- **Motivation:** Simple "did I read this?" lookup and a curated want-to-read list.

## 5. Go-to-Market Channels

### Phase 1 — Organic Foundation (Months 1–3)

**Content marketing**
- Weekly blog posts targeting high-intent search terms: "goodreads alternative 2026," "book tracking app," "how to track books you've read," "best book club apps."
- Each post ends with a direct import-from-Goodreads CTA — the lowest-friction migration path.

**Reddit & community seeding**
- Active presence in: r/books, r/bookclub, r/52book, r/suggestmeabook, r/BooksAreAwesome.
- Approach: be helpful first. Answer questions, then mention BookTracker when genuinely relevant.
- Share import success stories ("I moved 400 Goodreads books in 60 seconds").

**TikTok / Instagram Reels**
- Format: quick screen recordings — "Watch me import 5 years of Goodreads in 30 seconds," "My reading stats after 1 year of tracking."
- Tag: #BookTok, #ReadingTracker, #GoodreadsAlternative, #BookClub.

**ProductHunt launch**
- Target top-5 Product of the Day; coordinate upvotes from existing users.
- Offer a "Launch Week" bonus (e.g., exclusive stats export or early access to a new feature).

### Phase 2 — Referral & Clubs Flywheel (Months 3–6)

**Book club virality**
- Every club invite email is a product touchpoint for a non-user. Design the invite landing page to convert non-members: "You've been invited to [Club Name] on BookTracker. Join free →"
- Referral incentive: invite 3 friends → unlock a premium stats view.

**Goodreads migration campaign**
- Build a dedicated landing page: "Switch from Goodreads in 60 seconds."
- Run lightweight retargeting ads on Reddit and Meta to readers who have visited Goodreads-alternative content.

**Email newsletter**
- Monthly "Reading Wrapped" email: personal stats digest for each user — books finished, pages read, top genres. Highly shareable, keeps the app top-of-mind between sessions.

### Phase 3 — Paid & Partnership (Months 6–12)

**Search ads**
- Google/Bing: "goodreads alternative," "book tracking app," "book club app."
- Tight match types; send to the migration landing page.

**BookTok creator partnerships**
- Identify 5–10 mid-tier BookTok creators (50K–500K followers) with engaged audiences.
- Offer a free premium tier + affiliate link for a 30-day sponsored slot.
- Focus on the stats and import story — highly visual, low learning curve to demo.

**Library & university pilots**
- Approach library systems and university reading programs with a free group license.
- Provides credibility, word-of-mouth from librarians, and a low-CAC acquisition channel.

## 6. Growth Metrics (North Star Framework)

| Metric | Definition | Target (Month 6) |
|---|---|---|
| **Activated users** | Users who add ≥ 5 books within 7 days of sign-up | 40% of new sign-ups |
| **D30 retention** | Users active in the app 30 days after sign-up | 35% |
| **Club creation rate** | Users who create or join a club within 30 days | 15% of active users |
| **Import conversion** | Users who import ≥ 10 books via CSV/Goodreads | 25% of sign-ups |
| **Referrals per user** | Club invites sent per active user per month | 1.5 |

## 8. Competitive Response Plan

**If Goodreads launches a major redesign:**
- Lean into privacy angle — BookTracker data is *yours*, not Amazon's.
- Double down on book clubs as a differentiator Goodreads has consistently failed to build well.

**If StoryGraph adds clubs:**
- Compete on UX quality and mobile experience (barcode scan, import speed).
- Launch a "Switch from StoryGraph" campaign mirroring the Goodreads migration page.

**If a well-funded competitor emerges:**
- Focus on the German-speaking market (EN + DE) as a defensible niche where US competitors rarely invest.
- Expand language support (French, Spanish) to build a multilingual moat.

---

## 9. Localization & International Strategy

BookTracker ships with German support from launch — a deliberate head start in the DACH market (Germany, Austria, Switzerland, ~100M German speakers).

**Near-term (6 months):** Establish organic presence in German-speaking reading communities (e.g., LovelyBooks.de, Buchverein Reddit, GoodReads German groups).

**Mid-term (12 months):** Add French and Spanish translations to cover Western Europe and Latin America — markets underserved by English-first reading apps.

**Long-term:** Japanese and Korean readers are among the world's most active — high-value targets once the platform is mature.

## 10. Pricing Strategy

| Tier | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0/mo | Unlimited books, 1 club, core stats, import |
| **Reader Pro** | $3.99/mo or $29/yr | Unlimited clubs, advanced stats, export, reading goals |
| **Club Bundle** | $7.99/mo per club | Club owner pro features, member management, discussion digest emails |

**Rationale:**
- Free tier must be genuinely good — the import and library are the hook; limiting them kills word of mouth.
- Pro pricing is below a monthly coffee — low enough to be impulse-purchased by an engaged reader.
- Club Bundle creates a B2B-lite revenue stream: book clubs, libraries, and corporate wellness programs pay as a group.
